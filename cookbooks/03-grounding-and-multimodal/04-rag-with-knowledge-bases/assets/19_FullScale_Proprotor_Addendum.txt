                                           Summary
The Tiltrotor Test Rig (TTR) is a large-scale proprotor test system for the National Full-Scale
Aerodynamics Complex (NFAC). The first wind-tunnel entry was completed in November 2018.
The rotor tested was the Bell Model 699, an AW609 rotor modified specifically for wind-tunnel
testing. The TTR and rotor were tested up to 273 knots in axial flow, which is the highest airspeed
ever achieved by a full-scale proprotor in any wind tunnel. Extensive conversion-mode and
hover/climb data were also acquired. Previous reports presented results for all test configurations,
including numerical data for hover/climb conditions. The axial-flow and conversion-mode
numerical data used for those reports are presented herein.

                                          Background
The first entry of the Tiltrotor Test Rig (TTR) into the National Full-Scale Aerodynamics Complex
(NFAC) was completed in November 2018. It was considered a checkout test focused on
operational safety and efficiency, but the opportunity was used to collect rotor performance, loads,
and acoustics data for research. The checkout test used the Bell Model 699 rotor, which was built
specifically for NASA by Bell and derived from the right-hand rotor of the Leonardo AW609.
Figure 1 shows the TTR and 699 rotor in the NFAC.

Airplane-mode (axial flow), conversion-mode, and helicopter-mode (edgewise) conditions are the
focus of the present paper. Reference 1 presents results of these tests in the form of performance
and loads plots. The present report presents sufficient numerical data to recreate the plots in Ref.
1, supplemented with traditional rotor test data not previously published. Low-speed vertical climb
(near hover) data were previously published in Refs. 2-3.

Development of the TTR/699 is described in Ref. 4 and the test program is described in Refs. 1-3
and 5-6. References 7-8 document analytical studies of TTR/699 aeroelastic stability,
performance, and airloads. References 9-10 present correlations of measured TTR/699 loads and
performance with analytical predictions.

Reference 1 is a revision to an earlier paper, Ref. 11. All TTR/699 data were reprocessed for Ref.
1; key differences include thermally corrected rotor-balance data and aerodynamic tare corrections
throughout. The present report presents only the most recently processed TTR/699 data including
all corrections.

turntable, so rotor thrust is always positive. The rotor data reported here use the “B3” balance
calibration translated to the hub center. The wind-tunnel scales were locked, so all rotor loads were

Most data were taken with the rotor trimmed to either Mtip=0.693 or Mtip= 0.583. Within a given

parameters are highlighted in boldface.

The data plotted in Ref. 1 were not always collected in continuous parameter sweeps. The data
tables presented here have numerous gaps in data point numbers; omitted data points include
control-system checks, data-system diagnostics, operator training, etc., where either the wind
tunnel or the rotor were not trimmed consistent with other plotted data. Such data points are not
appropriate for parameter sweeps and were accordingly not plotted in Ref. 1 nor are they included
here. Run 76 is omitted here because the data quality was poor.
