                                                    Extended Abstract

     This paper discusses the Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) outputs of the Unsteady Pressure-Sensitive Paint
(uPSP) measurements, which were collected with four Phantom high-speed cameras at a constant sample frequency
in the Ascent Transient Aerodynamics Test (ATAT) of the Space Launch System (SLS) Block 1 cargo vehicle in the
11-by-11-foot transonic test section of the Unitary Plan Wind Tunnel (UPWT) at NASA Ames Research Center in
September 2019. Fig. 1 shows the ATAT scale model installed in the wind tunnel with uPSP applied to the model
surface.

     The DMD is based on the eigendecomposition of a best-fit linear operator that approximates the dynamics present
in the data. Given the timeseries of data, DMD computes a set of modes, each of which is associated with a fixed
oscillation frequency and growth/decay rate (Refs. [1] and [2]). The conventional DMD algorithm is based on the
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). For the data with zero mean, the DMD is equivalent to the Discrete Fourier
Transform (DFT) (Refs. [3] and [4]). Since the uPSP is mainly used to determine the unsteady property of the
aerodynamic flow, the DMD of the uPSP measurements is implemented in two steps (Ref. [5]): (1) subtract the mean
value from the uPSP measurement; (2) apply the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) on the resulting data with zero mean.

     The uPSP measurements collected with the Phantom cameras were projected to the grid nodes on the surface of
the scale model, and the first 1,024 frames were processed and the DMD outputs were generated. As an example, the
DMD outputs of a test of the SLS ATAT are shown below. The Mach number of the test was 0.73, and the angle of
attack and the angle of the sideslip were both zeros.

     Fig. 2 shows the average Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the timeseries of uPSP measurements on the grid nodes
in the test, in which the peaks corresponding to the fundamental component and the 1st harmonic of the vortex shedding
can be observed. The vortex shedding was generated when the flow passed the connection between the core and the
Solid Rocket Boosters (SRBs) of the SLS. In Figs. 3(a) and 3(b), the DMD modes at the vortex shedding frequency,
corresponding to the real and imaginary parts of the DFT coefficients, are visualized on the surface of the model. Two
waves originated at the connection between the core and the SRBs can be observed in the DMD modes, traveling in
upstream and downstream directions respectively.

    Fig. 4 shows the phase of the DMD modes at the vortex frequency, plotted in a plane of x coordinate and azimuth
angle of the grid nodes. The grid nodes with the phase close to -180o are plotted in blue and the grid nodes with the
phase close to +180o are plotted in yellow. It is obvious that the phase of the grid nodes located on the edge of the blue
and yellow regions is exactly -180o, or equivalently +180o. As shown in the figure, the two red lines, located upstream
and downstream respectively referenced to the connection between the core and the SRBs, link the grid nodes on the
edge of the blue and yellow regions along the streamline. Therefore, the lengths of the two red lines are equal to the

     Further investigations show the computed propagation velocity of the wave traveling in the upstream direction is
approximately equal to the difference of the speed of the sound (denoted as c) and the speed of the free stream flow
of the test (denoted as vfreeStream). Considering the propagation velocity of the wave is measured in a reference frame
fixed to the wind tunnel, its propagation velocity is approximately equal to the speed of the sound c in a reference
frame fixed to the free stream flow. Therefore, the wave observed in the DMD modes at the vortex shedding frequency,
traveling in the upstream direction, is actually acoustic wave. The computed propagation velocity of the wave traveling
in the downstream direction is approximately 79% of vfreeStream, and the value is consistent with the aerodynamic
convection velocity estimated.

     In this paper, the effectiveness to use the DMD outputs of uPSP measurements in the diagnosis and analysis of
the aerodynamic and acoustic phenomena in the SLS ATAT is demonstrated. The work described in the paper is a
part of NASA’s development of a new state-of-the-art uPSP capability in production wind tunnels. Funding was
provided by the NASA Aerosciences Evaluation and Test Capabilities (AETC) Project.
